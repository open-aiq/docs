You'll need:

- an ESP32-S3 board with at least 4 MB of flash (we use the DevKitC-1)
- a Plantower **PMS5003** particulate sensor
- a Sensirion **SHT30** temperature and humidity sensor
- a 128×64 I²C **OLED**, with either an SH1106 (default) or SSD1306 controller
- three push buttons (left, right and pair), a breadboard and some jumper
  wires
- optional: an RGB status LED (WS2812). Every ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 has one on
  board

Here's how it all connects. Pick the breadboard if you're wiring it by hand,
or Proto2 if you have the carrier board.

Don't skip SET. The firmware uses it to
[rest the particle sensor](../particle-sensor/) between samples. Left
unconnected, the sensor runs all the time and wears out sooner. RESET is
worth wiring too: if the sensor stops sending readings, the firmware pulses
it low to restart it. Without it, a stuck sensor stays stuck until you power
the monitor off and on.

This is the map for the Proto2 carrier board. If you wired a breadboard before
it, yours probably uses SDA 8, SCL 9, sensor TX 18, sensor RX 17 and buttons 4
(right) and 5 (left). Rewire it, or keep using firmware from before the change.

- **I²C:** the OLED and SHT30 share one 400 kHz bus at 3.3 V, and it needs
  pull-up resistors. Most breakouts already have them. If yours don't, add
  4.7 kΩ from SDA and from SCL to 3.3 V, like Proto2's R1 and R2.
- **PMS5003:** it needs 5 V power, and its UART runs at 9600 baud with 3.3 V
  logic. Cable and adapter pin orders vary, so wire by the labels.
- **Status LED:** the firmware sends the same signal to GPIO 48 and 38, so the
  devkit's LED lights on either board revision. Leave both pins free.
- **Buttons:** wire to ground; the chip's internal pull-ups do the rest.

At boot, the firmware looks for the SHT30 at `0x44` or `0x45` and the OLED at
`0x3C` or `0x3D`. If a part is missing, it's logged and the rest keeps working.
The OLED controller can't be detected, so choose it at build time
(`OLED_CONTROLLER=sh1106` or `ssd1306`) or grab the matching release image.

`src/board.rs` in the device repository is the source of truth for pins. The
board itself, with schematic, Gerbers and BOM, is in
[open-aiq/hardware](https://github.com/open-aiq/hardware). Stuck on wiring?
Ask us in a [GitHub issue](https://github.com/open-aiq/device/issues).

## Buttons

There are three: right, left and pair.

| Press | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Right | Next screen: AQI, Readings, Network |
| Left | Previous screen |
| Hold pair for 3 s | Opens Bluetooth pairing for 5 minutes. Holding it again restarts the 5 minutes |

On a breadboard, wire the pair button from GPIO 7 to ground, like the other
two. Without one, you can still open pairing by typing `BTN:PAIR` in the
[serial console](../firmware/#serial-console).

## Flash layout

`partitions.csv` holds two 1.94 MB app slots (`ota_0`, `ota_1`), which enable
[over-the-air updates](../ota/) with rollback. It fits any ESP32-S3 module with
4 MB of flash or more. Provisioning settings live in `nvs` and survive updates.
